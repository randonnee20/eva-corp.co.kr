Why causal inference?

Moving together is not the same as causing the change.

Even if targets that received an action seem to have better outcomes, it may be because their conditions were favorable to begin with. If factors that also affect the outcome, such as how targets were selected, the operating environment, timing, and target characteristics, are not taken into account, the effect may be overestimated or underestimated. Causal inference aligns cases with and without the action under comparable conditions to estimate the change produced by the action itself.

Factors that also affect the outcome

  • Selection of action targets
  • Operating environment
  • Timing
  • Target characteristics

Connecting data and knowledge

When data is connected, the context behind decisions becomes visible.

Business data is scattered across systems, documents, sensors, and reports. Causal AI connects targets, conditions, actions, and outcomes on a common basis, and organizes the action records and decision rationale people leave behind into a structure that can be analyzed. This makes it possible to see “which action was applied under which conditions, and what outcome it led to.”

An action effect does not end with a single estimate. We present the comparison condition, data scope, estimation interval, and validation results together so the analysis can be reviewed and judged.

The role of AI

AI provides the evidence; people make the final call.

Analytical figures and comparative evidence are produced from predefined rules and analysis results. Generative AI then organizes the context, interpretation, and recommendation text based on those results. Roles are separated so that the same data always yields the same figures and AI never generates numbers arbitrarily.

From the questions of predictive AI to the questions of Causal AI

As data grows, so does the number of indicators that move together. But the fact that they changed together does not, on its own, tell you whether one is the cause or a result produced by other conditions. Causal AI structures the relationship between actions and outcomes and, through effect estimation that accounts for differences in conditions, provides the evidence needed for decision-making.
